## 🌌 What Is ModeAtlas Core?

ModeAtlas Core is an ambitious reimagining of how humans traverse information. Where traditional repositories store code in static vaults, ModeAtlas Core treats every dataset, every module, and every operational mode as a **coordinate on an explorable map**. Think of it less as a software package and more as a **compass for the wilderness of structured knowledge** — a navigational instrument that lets you wander through modes, switch perspectives, and chart your own paths across a sprawling territory of functionality.

Inspired by the original `mode-atlas` project concept, this new repository ventures far beyond a simple mode-switching utility. It is a full **atlas framework**: a toolkit for defining regions of behavior, stitching them into navigable graphs, and exposing them through a responsive, multilingual, always-available interface. Whether you are orchestrating a fleet of microservices, mapping user journeys, or visualizing the hidden topology of a large configuration system, ModeAtlas Core gives you the cartographer's pen and the explorer's lantern.

The philosophy is simple yet radical: **modes should not be hidden menus — they should be lands you can walk.** Every togglable state, every environment variant, every runtime profile becomes a labeled province on the atlas, complete with its own metadata, relationships, and traversal rules. Users do not "click a dropdown"; they **travel**.

## 🧩 The Core Metaphor — Regions, Roads, and Waypoints

To understand ModeAtlas Core, you must first internalize its vocabulary:

- **Region** — a self-contained mode of operation. Light mode, dark mode, high-contrast mode, read-only mode, batch-processing mode. Each is a region with a distinct climate.
- **Road** — a transition path connecting two regions. Roads carry metadata: cost, latency, reversibility, permissions.
- **Waypoint** — a snapshot within a region. Waypoints let you bookmark positions and return to them with graceful restoration.
- **Cartographer** — you. The developer or operator who defines the atlas, refines its topology, and publishes it for others to explore.
- **Explorer** — the end user who navigates the atlas, switching modes, discovering waypoints, and personalizing their journey.

This metaphor is not merely decorative — it drives the entire architecture. The engine reasons in graphs, stores coordinates, and renders maps. The result is a system that scales from a tiny two-mode toggle all the way to a sprawling constellation of hundreds of interconnected operational states.

## ✨ Feature Constellation

Below is the full constellation of capabilities that ModeAtlas Core brings to your project. Each feature has been crafted with the explorer in mind.

### 🗺️ Atlas Construction & Graph Topology
- **Declarative Region Definitions** — describe your modes in a human-readable manifest, and the engine generates the navigable graph automatically.
- **Weighted Roads** — assign cost, latency, or risk to transitions; the engine can suggest optimal paths through your mode landscape.
- **Sub-Atlases** — nest atlases within atlases, allowing modular composition of complex systems into coherent wholes.
- **Topology Validation** — catch unreachable regions, dead-end roads, and circular traps before they reach your users.
- **Region Inheritance** — derive new regions from existing ones, overriding only what differs, for a remarkably tidy configuration surface.

### 🎨 Responsive UI & Visualization
- **Responsive Canvas Renderer** — the atlas stretches gracefully from a smartwatch to a wall-sized display, adapting layout, density, and typography in real time.
- **SVG & Canvas Output Modes** — choose vector precision for crisp zooming or canvas throughput for massive graphs with thousands of nodes.
- **Theming Tokens** — every visual property is exposed as a token, so your atlas can wear any brand's colors like a tailored garment.
- **Accessibility-First Design** — full keyboard navigation, screen-reader announcements for region transitions, and a dedicated high-contrast region built in.
- **Motion Preferences** — respects reduced-motion settings, replacing animated road transitions with instant, dignified shifts.

### 🌐 Multilingual Support
- **28 Languages Included** — from Arabic to Vietnamese, with right-to-left layout mirroring handled automatically.
- **Locale-Aware Formatting** — numbers, dates, and region names render according to the explorer's locale conventions.
- **Live Language Switching** — explorers can pivot languages mid-journey without losing their position on the atlas.
- **Community Translation Pipeline** — a structured workflow for contributors to add new languages without touching core code.

### ⚡ Performance & Scale
- **Lazy Region Loading** — distant provinces of the atlas are loaded on demand, keeping initial startup light.
- **Incremental Graph Diffing** — when the atlas changes, only the altered roads and regions are re-rendered.
- **Web Worker Offloading** — heavy pathfinding and layout computation run off the main thread, keeping the UI silky.
- **Memory-Aware Caching** — the engine monitors its own footprint and prunes stale waypoints automatically.

### 🔐 Security & Governance
- **Region Permission Scopes** — restrict who may travel to which regions, with role-based and attribute-based policies.
- **Audit Trails** — every transition can be logged with timestamp, actor, and rationale, forming a travel diary for compliance.
- **Signed Atlases** — publish your atlas with a cryptographic signature so explorers know it has not been tampered with in transit.
- **Sandboxed Regions** — run untrusted mode logic in isolated enclaves, preventing a rogue region from affecting the rest of the map.

### 🛠️ Developer Experience
- **Typed Schemas** — every manifest is validated against a rich schema, catching typos and misconfigurations at authoring time.
- **Hot Atlas Reloading** — edit your atlas and see it redraw instantly, without restarting your application.
- **CLI Cartographer Tools** — inspect, lint, diff, and visualize atlases from the command line.
- **Plugin Roads** — extend the engine with custom transition logic, from cloud API calls to hardware handshakes.

### 📞 24/7 Customer Support
- **Round-the-Clock Response Desk** — our support constellation never sleeps, ensuring explorers on any timezone receive timely guidance.
- **In-Atlas Help Waypoints** — drop a help waypoint into any region, and explorers can summon contextual assistance without leaving their journey.
- **Diagnostic Bundles** — one action produces a redacted, shareable snapshot of your atlas state for faster troubleshooting.
- **Community Forum Beacon** — a permanent channel where cartographers and explorers exchange maps, tips, and tales.

### 🧠 Intelligent Assistance
- **Path Suggestions** — the engine learns common journeys and proposes shortcuts that respect your governance rules.
- **Anomaly Detection** — unusual transition patterns are flagged for review, helping you spot misuse or emergent behavior.
- **Auto-Layout Heuristics** — let the engine arrange your regions aesthetically when you would rather not place them by hand.

## 🚀 The Explorer's Quickstart (No Terminal Incantations Required)

We deliberately avoid the usual command-line rituals. Instead, the intended entry point is a **visual onboarding atlas** shipped with the repository. Open the onboarding document, follow the labeled regions, and you will have a running instance charted within minutes.

For those who prefer textual guidance, the repository includes a **Provisions Guide** (`docs/provisions/`) that walks through:

1. Assembling your first manifest by describing two regions and one road.
2. Mounting the renderer into a host page or application shell.
3. Enabling the multilingual layer and selecting your starting locale.
4. Publishing your atlas to a static host or embedding it in an existing runtime.
5. Connecting the audit trail to your observability pipeline.

Each step is framed as a leg of a journey, with waypoints you can bookmark and revisit. The guide assumes no prior familiarity with graph theory, only a willingness to explore.

## 🧭 Atlas Manifest — A Glimpse at the Shape of Things

An atlas manifest is a declarative description of your mode landscape. Below is an illustrative fragment showing two regions and the road between them. Note the metadata-rich style — every element carries intent.

```
atlas:
  name: nebula-console
  version: 2026.1
  defaultRegion: daylight
  regions:
    - id: daylight
      label:
        en: Daylight Console
        fr: Console Diurne
        ja: 昼光コンソール
      tokens:
        background: "#ffffff"
        foreground: "#101418"
    - id: midnight
      label:
        en: Midnight Console
        fr: Console de Minuit
        ja: 真夜中コンソール
      tokens:
        background: "#0b0f14"
        foreground: "#e6edf3"
  roads:
    - from: daylight
      to: midnight
      cost: 1
      reversible: true
      animation: crossfade
```

The engine reads this, validates it against the schema, and produces a navigable atlas complete with keyboard shortcuts, locale switching, and audit hooks. You wrote a map; the engine built a world.

## 🌍 Multilingual Support in Depth

Language is not a coat of paint applied at the end; it is woven into the atlas fabric from the start. The engine ships with 28 locale bundles and a clear extension path:

- **Arabic (ar)** — full RTL mirroring of roads and labels.
- **Chinese, Simplified (zh-Hans)** and **Traditional (zh-Hant)** — distinct bundles honoring regional conventions.
- **English (en)**, **French (fr)**, **German (de)**, **Spanish (es)**, **Portuguese (pt)** — core Western European set.
- **Hindi (hi)**, **Bengali (bn)**, **Tamil (ta)** — South Asian coverage with proper script shaping.
- **Japanese (ja)**, **Korean (ko)** — CJK typography rules respected.
- **Swahili (sw)**, **Yoruba (yo)**, **Zulu (zu)** — African language representation.
- **And more** — the full list lives in the locale manifest.

Adding a language is a well-trodden road: copy the template, translate the strings, submit a pull request. The pipeline validates completeness and renders a preview atlas in your new language.

## 🛡️ Governance and Permissions

Not every explorer should wander every region. ModeAtlas Core provides a layered permission model:

- **Role-Based Scopes** — assign roles such as `visitor`, `operator`, `curator`, and map them to regions.
- **Attribute-Based Rules** — express policies like "regions tagged `sensitive` require an active session younger than 30 minutes."
- **Quarantine Regions** — isolate experimental regions so they are unreachable unless explicitly unlocked.
- **Transition Auditing** — optionally require a rationale note for roads into high-stakes regions.

Policies are declared alongside the atlas, keeping governance close to the map it governs.

## 🧬 Extensibility — Growing Your Own Provinces

The plugin system allows cartographers to extend the engine without forking it. Plugins can:

- Define **custom road types** with bespoke transition animations.
- Register **region resolvers** that fetch region definitions from remote sources.
- Add **renderer adapters** for novel output surfaces such as terminal UIs or e-ink displays.
- Hook into the **lifecycle events** emitted before, during, and after a region transition.

A plugin registry is planned, and the community is encouraged to publish their provinces for others to explore.
